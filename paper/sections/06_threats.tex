\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

Every result in Section~\ref{sec:results} depends on choices a reader cannot see
in the numbers. This section states them, ordered by how far each could move a
conclusion, and names the one that could move the most first.

\subsection{The Control-Traffic Subsidy}
\label{sec:subsidy}

Centralized protocols are charged nothing for the uplink that informs the sink
of node state. This is the largest single confound in the study and it favors
five of the ten configurations. The justification is quantitative: a separate
200-bit status report per node per round, over a sink distance above 100~m and
therefore on the fourth-power branch, costs roughly $3.6\times10^{-5}$~J per
node per round, about a quarter of the entire 1~J budget across the round cap.
Charging it would sink all five centralized protocols for a reason unconnected
to whether their clustering decisions are any good, and the piggyback assumption
is standard in this literature. But a defensible modeling choice is still a
choice, and its magnitude belongs in the results rather than in a footnote.

Measured over the full sweep, LEACH spends 12.73\% of its consumed energy on
control traffic against 2.33\% for the fuzzy system, 2.46\% for NSGA-II, 2.38\%
for the map, 3.95\% for the DQN and 3.85\% for the GCN, so the subsidy is worth
roughly nine to ten percentage points of the total budget. In packet terms the
gap is starker, 171{,}644 control packets for LEACH against 9{,}657 to 12{,}216
for the centralized five, but that overstates the effect, since control packets
are a fifth the size of data packets and most are short-range.

Two consequences follow. A claim of the form ``NSGA-II extends first death by
80\% over LEACH'' conflates clustering quality with an accounting convention and
should not be made without qualification. And the reliable comparisons here are
the within-class ones, centralized against centralized and distributed against
distributed, with the chain protocol comparing cleanly to neither. The strongest
results in Section~\ref{sec:results}, the channel-conditional retraction and the
DQN-versus-GCN rotation contrast, are both within-class and unaffected. The
clean resolution is an ablation that charges the uplink honestly and re-runs the
sweep, which would convert this caveat into a measurement. It has not been run.

\subsection{Transmit Power Is Bracketed, Not Swept}

Section~\ref{sec:retraction} treats the channel comparison as a result, which it
is, but two configurations bracket a continuum without tracing it. A transmit
power between the endpoints, or above the lossy one, would produce intermediate
behavior this study does not measure, and a three-point or four-point sweep
would show where the fuzzy system's and the DQN's advantage actually crosses the
significance threshold. That sweep has not been run. The scale sweep partially
compensates, since moving the sink changes how much of the population sits in
the waterfall, but it is not a substitute: field size changes area and channel
severity together, so it locates the boundary without isolating the parameter.
The path-loss exponent, shadowing standard deviation, noise floor and retry
budget are equally unspecified in the source material and equally consequential.
We used conventional values for 2.4~GHz outdoor short-range
links~\cite{rappaport2002wireless,ieee802154} and published the resulting
packet-error curve so the choice is auditable, but we did not vary them.

\subsection{The Pre-Training Asymmetry}

The DQN and the GCN arrive pre-trained; the other seven do not. Their reported
setup time and operation counts are inference costs only, and training is real,
paid once and reported separately, so it must not be read as free. The seven
non-learned protocols receive no equivalent offline preparation, which makes
this a fair comparison of a deployed system and not an apples-to-apples
algorithmic comparison; both statements are true and which matters depends on
the question being asked.

Generalization is now measured rather than assumed, but only in one direction.
The evaluation seeds are disjoint from the training seeds, so the headline
result is not memorization, and the scale sweep carries the frozen weights into
eight cells the policies never saw without either collapsing. What that does not
establish is how these policies would compare against retrained versions of
themselves, or how they behave under a sink placement not geometrically similar
to the training one, since every cell keeps the base station at $(W/2, 1.5W)$.

\subsection{Modeling Simplifications}

The radio model accounts for transmit, receive and fusion energy and nothing
else: no idle listening, no sleep-wake transitions, no radio startup, no
processor energy, and a linear reservoir with no rate-capacity or recovery
effect. Idle listening frequently dominates real deployments and including it
would compress the differences between all ten configurations toward zero.

Fusion is ideal: a head receiving any number of member packets emits exactly
one, so compression is lossless and free in bits. This systematically favors
high-fan-in structures and PEGASIS most of all, whose aggregation ratio of 80
readings per delivered packet is exactly the quantity the assumption idealizes,
and it shapes the chain-versus-cluster comparison more than any other single
simplification.

There is no MAC layer, so scheduling is perfect and collision-free and packet
loss comes only from distance-dependent
error~\cite{zhao2003understanding,woo2003taming}. Adding interference would
penalize dense-cluster protocols relative to the chain and would make the number
of simultaneously active heads a variable this study cannot see, and latency
inherits the same limitation, so its absolute figures are lower bounds whose
ordering is meaningful and whose magnitudes are not. Everything is static, and
the head-to-sink hop is single-hop for nine of ten configurations, matching each
protocol's original specification and leaving the chain as the sole multi-hop
contrast. Sensor data is a mean-reverting process rather than a real trace, so
the reactive protocols' results are conditional on that model. Finally, every
protocol targets the same nominal head count but realizes it differently, and
LEACH's realized count is deliberately left stochastic, so part of every
observed difference is attributable to realized cluster count rather than to
selection logic.

\subsection{Scope and Non-Calibration}

This study establishes results at one energy budget and one traffic model.
Network scale is swept over three node counts and three field areas, but within
a single family of geometrically similar deployments, and three protocols have
complexity superlinear in node count. Node energy is homogeneous, every node
senses every round with no duty cycling or query workload, and there is no
hardware testbed and no measured trace; every number is simulation output under
the assumptions above.

No protocol was tuned to reproduce a published figure. Only protocol
descriptions, the radio model and simulation parameters were taken from prior
work, and its numeric results were discarded before implementation began, so
disagreement with published tables is expected and is not evidence of error
here. The one place this commitment was tested is documented in
Section~\ref{sec:dqn-disclosure}, where the DQN's discount factor and training
length were revised after an observed divergence, after which a stopping rule
was fixed in advance and the result recorded as final regardless of outcome. We
state this because the alternative, presenting the final hyperparameters as if
they had been the first, is the ordinary way such revisions disappear from a
methods section~\cite{henderson2018matters,pineau2021reproducibility}.
