\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Paired Trials}

Every protocol is evaluated over 30 trials in each channel configuration, 600
runs in the headline study, and the trials are paired rather than independent.
Trial index $i$ seeds a single random stream generating, in fixed order, the
node positions, the initial energies, the full per-link shadowing matrix and the
sensed-value array for all rounds. That order depends only on $i$ and never on
which protocol is running, so at a given index all ten configurations face a
byte-identical physical world. Protocol-internal randomness comes from a
separate stream derived by a keyed hash of run index and protocol name; Python's
built-in hash is salted per process and is never used anywhere in the simulator,
since it would silently destroy reproducibility across invocations.

Pairing licenses the statistical procedure below and is substantially stronger
than the unpaired mean-and-deviation comparison this literature reports:
topology variance is large relative to the differences between protocols, and an
unpaired test spends most of its power fighting variance a paired design
eliminates by construction. The per-packet channel draws are deliberately not
shared, since different protocols make different numbers of draws per round and
there is no coherent sense in which two could experience the same coin flips
packet by packet. The fairness-critical quantity is the static per-link
shadowing, which is identical.

\subsection{The Channel Sensitivity Pair}
\label{sec:channel-pair}

The entire study is run twice, once with the packet-error model of
Section~\ref{sec:channel} enabled and once with it disabled. This is not a
robustness appendix but a decision rule fixed before the results were read:

\begin{quote}
A conclusion that holds in both channel configurations is a statement about the
protocol. A conclusion that changes between them is a statement about transmit
power.
\end{quote}

Transmit power is a parameter the reference specifications do not fix, and
Section~\ref{sec:channel} showed the packet-error curve is a waterfall whose
position determines how severely single-hop protocols are penalized on the sink
hop. Choosing it to make a metric discriminating would be precisely the
calibration this study forbids, and running both endpoints removes the choice
from the argument. Section~\ref{sec:retraction} reports one conclusion the rule
retracts.

\subsection{The Node-Count and Field-Area Sweep}
\label{sec:scale-design}

A single operating point cannot tell a reader whether a result is about
clustering or about one deployment. We repeat the lossy study over a $3\times3$
grid of node counts $N \in \{50, 100, 150\}$ and square field sides
$W \in \{50, 100, 150\}$~m at 15 paired runs per cell, a further 1{,}350 runs.

The base station scales with the field at $(W/2, 1.5W)$, keeping the deployment
geometrically self-similar. This also means field size sets channel severity as
well as area: mean node-to-sink distance is 52~m at $50\times50$~m with no node
beyond 120~m, 104~m at $100\times100$~m with 33\% beyond, and 156~m at
$150\times150$~m with 75\% beyond. That is a real confound between bigger area
and worse channel, and we report mean sink distance per cell so a reader can
separate the two rather than engineering the confound away and losing
self-similarity instead. The density radius feature scales at $0.25W$, since
held fixed at 25~m it would cover 78\% of a $50\times50$ field and 9\% of a
$150\times150$ one, silently changing what the feature means; it is read
identically by every protocol, so scaling it is a shared environment choice
rather than per-protocol tuning. The DQN and GCN use their frozen $N = 100$,
$100\times100$ weights in every cell with no retraining, which makes the sweep a
measurement of out-of-distribution transfer and is why the untrained fuzzy
system matters as a control: a protocol with no training at all cannot suffer
transfer failure, so if it moves the same way the learned ones do, the cause is
the environment. Comparisons inside a cell stay paired; comparisons across cells
are not, because a different $N$ means a different number of nodes to seed, and
are not tested as if they were.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

Lifetime is reported at first, half and last node death plus the area under the
living-node curve, all four together because the three death points do not agree
on an ordering. Runs reaching the cap without full depletion are flagged as
censored, and a censored last-death figure is a lower bound, never averaged into
a mean without the censoring count beside it.

Delivery is reported in two units and the distinction matters. Packets received
at the sink measures aggregation depth, not delivery: one chain round delivers a
single fused packet where one clustered round delivers roughly five, so on a
packet basis the chain protocol appears five times worse while actually
delivering more readings. Original readings represented in delivered packets is
the cross-protocol comparable quantity, which is why delivery ratio here is
reading-based; the packet-based formula reported ten percent on a lossless
channel purely because ten source readings became one packet. Suppressed
readings count as losses in neither numerator nor denominator, since a reading
never injected was never lost, and the cost of suppression appears instead in
data yield, readings delivered over readings taken.

Computational cost is reported as wall-clock setup time, counted algorithmic
operations and analytical complexity, never merged, because wall-clock reflects
implementation quality as much as algorithm design. Timing and memory are
measured in separate runs, since allocation tracing inflated setup time by
factors between 3.5 and 7.8 depending on how many small objects a protocol
allocated, distorting the ranking rather than only the scale.

\subsection{Statistical Procedure and Verification}
\label{sec:stats}

Because trials are paired, each comparison against a reference reduces to a
vector of 30 per-topology differences. The null hypothesis that the difference
distribution is symmetric about zero is tested by a sign-flip permutation
test~\cite{good2005permutation} over 20{,}000 random sign vectors, with the
standard add-one correction so no $p$-value is reported as exactly zero; the
floor at 30 paired samples is $5\times10^{-5}$. The procedure was validated
against exhaustive enumeration on a reduced sample, giving an exact $p$ of
0.00391 against a sampled 0.00420. Confidence intervals come from 20{,}000
paired bootstrap resamples~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}, and because nine
comparisons are made against the reference within each metric and channel, all
$p$-values are corrected by Holm-Bonferroni~\cite{holm1979stepdown}, giving a
corrected floor of $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.00045$. Permutation was chosen over a
paired $t$-test because it assumes only exchangeability, not normality, and
several of these difference distributions are visibly skewed, the GCN's being
bimodal; and over Wilcoxon signed-rank because it operates on the actual
differences rather than their ranks, so the effect size and the test statistic
are the same quantity~\cite{demsar2006statistical}.

Seven gates were required to pass before any number here was read: energy
conservation asserted every run to $10^{-9}$~J against~\eqref{eq:conservation};
radio energies checked against hand computation on both sides of the crossover;
an invariant suite over energy, liveness, death ordering and delivery range;
determinism, which all nine protocols satisfy exactly across three repeats per
seed; a static audit across eighteen checks confirming no protocol writes to the
energy or liveness arrays or chooses its own packet sizes; independent
recomputation of one protocol's summary row; and the packet-error curve asserted
non-decreasing and strictly between zero and one somewhere in the band of sink
distances, without which the channel would be a binary range switch and the
lossy results would carry no information.

The headline sweep completes in 80 minutes on sixteen cores and the scale sweep
in 222 on fourteen, with figures from Matplotlib~\cite{hunter2007matplotlib} and
the raw per-round output of every run retained. The central cell of the scale
sweep reproduces the first 15 runs of the headline lossy study exactly for all
ten protocols, on both first node death and area under the living-node curve,
which is the check that the two harnesses are the same simulator.
